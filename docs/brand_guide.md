# Ruins Untold — Brand Guide

> Inject this file into any AI agent prompt that needs to produce content consistent with Ruins Untold brand. The sections below cover channel identity, narrator voice, visual style, typography, thumbnails, motion, and music.

---

## Channel Identity

**Ruins Untold** is a faceless, narration-driven documentary YouTube channel. Topics: ancient mysteries, forbidden archaeology, suppressed history, alternative history. No host. No face. No on-screen presence. The narrator is an invisible intelligence — calm, authoritative, and slightly unsettling. The audience is not watching a person. They are being guided through a controlled revelation.

**Core tension:** The official story is real — and it is incomplete. The channel occupies the disciplined middle ground between mainstream academia and fringe theory. Never conspiracy. Never debunking. Always investigation.

---

## Narrator Voice

### What the narrator is

- **Omniscient** — knows the full story before it is revealed
- **Measured** — never excited, never panicked; the gravity of the subject makes excitement unnecessary
- **Intelligent** — treats the audience as curious adults, not passive consumers
- **Cinematic** — thinks in scenes and images, not bullet points
- **Slightly ominous** — not theatrical; the ominous quality comes from what is *not* said as much as what is

### What the narrator is not

- Not a podcast host. No casual banter. No "guys," "honestly," or "like I said."
- Not a conspiracy theorist. The channel never presents speculation as fact.
- Not a lecturer. The script doesn't explain — it reveals.
- Not on camera. The narrator has no personal opinions, no visible identity.

### Quick Reference

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Person | Third-person omniscient — never first person |
| Tone | Measured, intelligent, slightly ominous |
| Register | Premium documentary narration — NOT podcast, NOT commentary |
| Pace | 145–160 WPM; short punches for impact, long sentences for atmosphere |
| Forbidden phrases | "I think," "I believe," "guys," "crazy," "insane," "smash that like button" |
| Preferred phrases | "the evidence suggests," "researchers have found," "some argue," "what history doesn't tell us" |
| Alternative theories | Always framed as interpretation: "some researchers argue," "one reading of the evidence holds" |
| Specificity rule | Always the exact number — "117 people" not "over a hundred" |

## Vocabulary Standards

### Language to use

| Context | Preferred Language |
|---|---|
| Expressing uncertainty | "the evidence suggests," "researchers have found," "what the record shows" |
| Introducing alternative theory | "some researchers argue," "one interpretation holds," "what if the answer is" |
| Describing anomalies | "cannot be explained by," "defies the conventional timeline," "the mainstream account leaves this unresolved" |
| Rhetorical questions | Ask them — then sit in them. Don't answer immediately. |
| Scale and specificity | Always prefer the specific number. "117 people" not "over a hundred." "1,400 years" not "more than a millennium." |

### Language to avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "I think," "I believe," "in my opinion" | The narrator has no first-person voice |
| "Smash that like button," "drop a comment" | Wrong register |
| "Crazy," "insane," "mind-blowing" | Cheap register; content earns impact through specificity |
| "Guys," "folks," "everyone" | Podcast banter; wrong format |
| "Allegedly," "supposedly" | Too dismissive; use "some researchers argue" instead |
| Hyperbolic absolutes ("nobody knows," "completely impossible") | Undercut credibility; use "no mainstream explanation accounts for" |

## Sentence Structure and Rhythm

Short sentences land impact. Longer sentences build atmosphere and carry the audience through a complex idea.

**Short sentences are weapons. Use them at the moment of maximum impact.**

> The ground is silent. The fires are out. One hundred and seventeen men, women, and children — gone.

**Long sentences are atmosphere. Use them to build a world before something breaks it.**

> The Antikythera Mechanism was found in a first-century Roman shipwreck, buried under two millennia of saltwater and sediment, and when the crust finally cracked open in an Athens storage room, what fell out was not a coin or a statue or a ceramic — it was a device of such mechanical sophistication that it would rewrite everything historians thought they knew about the ancient world.

**Rhythm target:** Mix 3–5 word punches with 30–50 word flowing passages. Never more than three long sentences in a row without a break.

## Episode Structure (9 parts)

| Part | Function | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Cold Open | Drop into the most compelling moment. No context. Pure tension. | Ominous, present tense, sensory |
| Channel Hook | Branded transition. "Welcome back to Ruins Untold…" Signal the deep-dive. | Measured, direct |
| Act 1 — World Before | Context. Make the audience care before anything goes wrong. | Epic, atmospheric, past tense |
| Act 2 — The Event | Something breaks. Slow the most dramatic beat down. End on the central mystery fully exposed. | Tense, cinematic, present tense |
| Act 3 — Official Story | Present the mainstream explanation fully and fairly. Never strawman it. | Measured, credible, academic |
| Act 4 — What They Won't Tell You | Alternative evidence. Lead with the anomaly that breaks the official story. Build methodically. | Unsettling, revelatory |
| Act 5 — Bigger Picture | Zoom out. Connect to a larger pattern. Challenge the audience's worldview. | Vast, philosophical |
| Conclusion | Return to Cold Open image. Restate the mystery. No definitive answer. Leave the question open. | Reflective, unresolved |
| Call to Action | Episode-specific comment question. Subscribe tied to channel mission. One-line next-episode tease. | Warm, still in narration register |

---

## Retention Mechanics (Built Into the Voice)

**Open loops:** Introduce a question or tension early that is not resolved until much later.

**Pattern interrupts:** Every 90–120 seconds, shift something — tone, location, scale, perspective. The voice never stays in one register too long.

**Cliffhanger act endings:** Every act ends on an unresolved tension.

**Specificity over generality:** The specific detail is always more powerful than the approximation. Specificity signals research. Research signals credibility. Credibility earns trust. Trust earns watch time.

---

## CTA Voice

Call to Action sections should feel like a continuation of the narration, not a break from it.

**Right:**
> What do you think was really inside that shipwreck — and who do you think built the device? Leave it below. If you want to keep digging, there's more where this came from. Next time: a site so anomalous that three separate governments have tried to restrict access to it.

**Wrong:**
> Smash that like button, subscribe, and hit the notification bell so you never miss a video!

### Typography Rules

- Thumbnails use two font layers only: headline (Anton, white) + keyword accent (Anton, Thumbnail Yellow)
- Never use more than two typefaces in a single composition
- Logo type is never modified, stretched, or replaced

## Thumbnail Design System

### Layouts

**Layout A — Hero + Inset Grid (primary)**
```
┌────────────────────────┬──────────┐
│                        │  inset 1 │
│   MAIN HERO IMAGE      ├──────────┤
│                        │  inset 2 │
│  HEADLINE TEXT         └──────────┤
│  KEYWORD TEXT          duration   │
└──────────────────────────────────┘
```
Left: ~65–70% width — primary documentary/ruins image. Right: 2 stacked inset images. Duration badge: lower right.

**Layout B — Full Bleed (for cosmic/atmospheric episodes)**

Single dramatic image fills the entire frame. Text overlaid with high contrast — white headline, yellow key phrase.

### Thumbnail Rules

- **Headline:** 3–7 words. White. Anton/Impact. ALL CAPS.
- **Keyword accent:** 1–3 words. Thumbnail Yellow. Same font.
- **Text placement:** Upper-left or lower-left. Never centered.
- **Headline language:** Question, claim, or revelation — never a summary.
  - Questions: "WHAT DID THEY FIND?" / "WHY DID IT VANISH?"
  - Claims: "SOMETHING ENDED THEM ALL" / "THIS WASN'T SUPPOSED TO EXIST"
  - Revelations: "1200 BC — SOMETHING CHANGED" / "THE RECORD STOPS HERE"
- **Arrows:** One per composition max. Bright yellow, pointing at key detail in inset.
- **"NEW" badge:** Alert Red, upper right — episode launch only; remove after 2 weeks.

### Thumbnail Color

Background dominated by Abyss / near-black. Hero image slightly desaturated, darkened at edges for text contrast. No gradients competing with text.

## Generated Image Visual Standards

### Core Style Directive

Include verbatim in all image prompts: `photorealistic, hyper-detailed, cinematic, documentary archaeology`

### Image Color

- **Dominant:** deep ochre, weathered stone gray, aged parchment, charcoal, burnt sienna
- **Accents (max 2):** amber torchlight, cold blue moonlight, dusty gold, deep crimson

Never prompt for: saturated primaries, pure white, neon, pastels, bright modern colors.

### Lighting Standards

| Setting | Lighting |
|---|---|
| Underground / tomb / cave | Warm torchlight from below-frame, cold blue accent on stone |
| Temple / interior | Shafts of directional sunlight through openings, torch fill |
| Exterior daytime | Dramatic directional sun, golden hour or harsh midday; long shadows |
| Exterior night | Cold moonlight dominant, scattered fire glow |
| Artifact close-up | Single raking light across surface to reveal texture |

Rule: **Never flat. Never even. Never studio.** Every light source has a direction, a color temperature, and a shadow.

### Generated Video Style

- No fast cuts, no handheld shake, no action-style motion
- Motion should feel like deep time — slow, inevitable, ancient

## Music Identity

Instrumental only. No lyrics. No vocals.

| Episode Phase | Character | Reference Style |
|---|---|---|
| Intro (Cold Open → Act 1) | Sparse, atmospheric, mysterious | Max Richter, Brian Eno ambient |
| Investigation (Acts 1–3) | Tense, methodical, building dread | Hans Zimmer documentary underscore |
| Revelation (Act 4) | Dramatic, dissonant, unsettling | Jóhann Jóhannsson, orchestral swell |
| Reflection (Act 5 → Conclusion) | Expansive, melancholic, philosophical | Ambient pads, solo strings, wide reverb |
| Outro (CTA) | Fading, unresolved, closing | Returns to intro texture; no cadence |

Volume: -20 dB relative to narration. Fade in over 3 seconds at start, fade out over 4 seconds at end. Music never cadences cleanly. The episode ends before the question does.
